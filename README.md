## Overview

The SCP Foundation is a collaborative writing project hosted at scp-wiki.wikidot.com (the SCP Wiki). Within its shared fiction, the Foundation is a secret international organisation that 'secures, contains, and protects' anomalous objects, entities, locations and phenomena that violate normal physical or biological laws, in order to prevent societal disruption. The project began on 22 June 2007 when an anonymous 4chan /x/ user later identified as Wesley 'Moto42' Williams posted SCP-173, an immobile concrete sculpture (referencing a real artwork by Izumi Kato) that kills any unobserved person within line-of-sight. Other anonymous writers responded with further faux-clinical containment files in the same format, and the corpus was migrated to its own EditThis wiki in early 2008 and then to the dedicated SCP Wiki on Wikidot in July 2008. The project has grown into one of the largest extant works of collaborative fiction, with the main SCP series now exceeding 9,000 numbered articles plus thousands of 'Tales' and translated branches in dozens of languages. SCPs are sorted by 'Object Class' (Safe, Euclid, Keter, Thaumiel, Apollyon, Neutralized, etc.) and by individual style; articles are formatted as redacted internal documents. All content is licensed CC BY-SA, which has enabled extensive video-game (SCP – Containment Breach, SCP: Secret Laboratory, Control), animation, tabletop and merchandise spin-offs. The project is widely studied as a paradigm of collaborative internet folklore.

## Theories & Explanations

### Scientific

**Collaborative folklore / shared-world fiction** _(Lincoln Michel, Lynne McNeill (Utah State University, digital folklore), various game-studies scholars)_

The SCP Foundation is studied as a paradigm of large-scale collaborative folklore enabled by Creative Commons licensing, distributed authorship, and wiki-based curation; its growth and mythopoetic structure have been analysed by scholars of digital folklore and game studies.

### Skeptical

**Fictional from the outset** _(SCP community itself)_

Unlike many creepypastas, the SCP Foundation has never been seriously claimed to be anything but fiction. Its skeptical analysis is therefore primarily literary and folkloric rather than evidential.
